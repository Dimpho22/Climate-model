Anopheles gambiae Climate Analysis

Project Overview

This project investigates the historical spread and eradication of Anopheles gambiae in Brazil, exploring whether climatic conditions contributed to the mosquito’s survival, distribution and eventual eradication.

Using CLIMEX, the project compares environmental conditions across selected locations to examine how rainfall, temperature and relative humidity may influence mosquito habitat suitability and breeding patterns.

Project Objectives

* Explore the historical spread and eradication of Anopheles gambiae in Brazil.
* Compare climatic conditions across selected regions.
* Examine the influence of rainfall, temperature and relative humidity on mosquito survival.
* Investigate how seasonal climate patterns may affect mosquito breeding and malaria risk.
* Evaluate the usefulness and limitations of climate-based modelling in understanding species distribution.

Methodology

The analysis used CLIMEX to examine climatic conditions in different geographical locations, including Natal, Dakar and Monte Santo.

The project considered:

* Ecoclimatic Index (EI).
* Annual rainfall.
* Relative humidity at 9 am and 3 pm.
* Minimum and maximum annual temperatures.

Climate graphs and geographical maps were used to compare environmental patterns. Historical information about mosquito eradication efforts in Brazil was also examined to interpret the findings.

Key Findings

The project explored how differences in rainfall, temperature and humidity could influence suitable breeding conditions for Anopheles gambiae.

The analysis considered how seasonal changes might affect mosquito reproduction and how climate information could support the identification of potentially suitable habitats and periods of increased malaria risk.
